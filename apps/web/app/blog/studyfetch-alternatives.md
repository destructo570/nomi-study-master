## Why Students Are Looking Beyond StudyFetch

StudyFetch has a lot going for it. The idea of uploading one file and getting flashcards, quizzes, notes, and a tutor chat is genuinely useful. The Spark.live community study sessions add a social dimension that most study apps lack.

However, for students preparing for exams, StudyFetch has a few critical gaps:

**No mind maps.** Mind maps help you see how concepts connect — essential for subjects like biology, medicine, law, and history. StudyFetch doesn't generate them.

**No podcasts.** Audio learning is one of the biggest innovations in study tech. StudyFetch doesn't offer AI-generated study podcasts.

**Weak spaced repetition.** StudyFetch has flashcards, but its spaced repetition isn't as robust as dedicated schedulers like Anki or RemNote. Without strong scheduling, material slips.

**Artifacts feel templated.** Common student feedback is that some StudyFetch artifacts feel generic — depth varies by subject, and the generation can feel one-size-fits-all.

The core issue is that StudyFetch generates artifacts but doesn't close the loop. It generates, but it doesn't retain. For exam preparation, retention is what matters.

---

## What Makes Nomi Different?

Nomi's design isn't just multi-artifact generation — it's a study loop built around retention.

The loop has four phases: **ingest → structure → test → remember**.

**Ingest:** Upload a PDF, paste notes, import a YouTube lecture. Nomi reads the material.

**Structure:** Nomi generates summaries, structured notes, and a mind map.

**Test:** Nomi generates flashcards and a quiz from the same source.

**Remember:** Spaced repetition surfaces the cards you're about to forget, right before you forget them. A study guide and podcast reinforce the material from different angles.

This loop means material doesn't just get generated once and abandoned — it gets reviewed, tested, and reinforced over time until it sticks.
