## Why Students Are Looking Beyond NotebookLM

NotebookLM has a genuinely impressive feature set for a free tool. Its Audio Overview podcast feature is delightful. Its source-grounded answers — where every response cites the specific passage in your uploaded document — set a standard for trustworthy AI.

However, for students, NotebookLM has a critical gap: **it's a research tool, not a study tool.**

The problem is simple: you can't test yourself. There are no flashcards, no quizzes, no spaced repetition, and no study guides. NotebookLM helps you understand material, but it doesn't help you retain it.

For students preparing for exams, understanding is only half the battle. The other half is retrieval practice — actively recalling information so it moves from short-term to long-term memory. Research consistently shows that retrieval practice improves long-term retention far more than passive reading or summarizing.

NotebookLM also lacks mind maps and has limited cross-notebook workflows. You upload sources into a notebook, ask questions, and get grounded answers — but you're largely left to your own devices for the actual studying part.

For students who need both understanding and retention, there's a better path.
